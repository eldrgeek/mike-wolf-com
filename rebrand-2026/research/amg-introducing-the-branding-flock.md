Baby flamingos are actually born grey

Did you know baby flamingos are actually born grey?

It takes 2–3 years for them to turn pink — gradually, as they grow and adapt to their environment. The colour isn’t given. It’s earned.

Most Substack creators are grey flamingos. Not because they lack taste or ambition — but because a strong visual brand doesn’t arrive on day one. It develops through repetition, consistency, and the right system.

I spent eight months figuring that out the hard way. Prompting, testing, failing, rebuilding. Post by post, I’ve been documenting everything in the Branding with AI series — the brand character post, the visual style development, the brand voice system, the step-by-step breakdowns. Your Visuals Are Either Winning Readers or Losing Them. In Two Seconds.

When someone lands on your Substack, they scan — and make a call: is this person legit? Your writing never gets a fair trial if your visuals lose the case first.

I know this because I lived it. I went from 64 to 200 subscribers in two weeks after my rebrand. Not because I wrote better. Because the visual system finally matched what I was actually saying.

Carrie Loranger has been gaining 1,000 subscribers a month since her rebrand. That’s not a coincidence — it’s the subconscious mechanism at work. Consistency of visuals signals reliability. Reliability signals trust. Trust converts.

The data from my own content tells the same story:

my image notes get 1.9x more views than text-only notes.

My video covers outperform static covers on open rates.

These are psychological responses: color, composition, and visual style are signals your audience’s brain decodes automatically, before a single word gets read.

Most creators pick colors they like and call it a brand. The real work is building a system that translates who you actually are. A brand is a system your audience can recognize before they see your name.

AI makes this harder and easier at the same time. It makes visual creation accessible to anyone. But it doesn’t make it consistent, it just makes the gap between “looks good once” and “looks like me every time” more visible.

That’s the problem The Branding Flock solves.
