Track 2: You Don’t Have a Brand Yet. Don’t Skip This Part.

If you opened Claude Design and froze at the first field — you’re not behind, you’re just missing the foundation. And without it, Claude Design will give you something generic. Visually confused. Not really you.

5 steps before you start building in Claude Design.

Here’s what to gather before you touch Claude Design:

1. Write your company blurb.

Not a tagline. A description of what you do, who you’re for, and what you want to feel like visually. One specific sentence.

Weak: “I’m a content creator.” Strong: “AI Meets Girlboss: a Substack for ambitious women building personal brands with AI — visual, witty, and stylish, never corporate.”

Claude uses this to make every aesthetic decision. Don’t wing it.

2. Decide on three colors.

Primary, neutral, accent. Hex codes if you have them. A description if you don’t: “warm coral, off-white, deep teal.”

3. Name your fonts.

One for headings, one for body. If you don’t have a preference: describe the personality. “Modern and geometric for headings, clean and readable for body.”

4. Write one style sentence.

This is the step most people skip — and the one that makes the biggest difference.

Structure: “[Visual style] with a [mood] — always [what you want], never [what you don’t].”

Example: “Mixed-media fashion illustration with a handmade editorial feel — always warm and stylish, never corporate or generic.”

5. Add your Substack or website URL.

Even if you hate how it currently looks. Claude reads it for context. Context beats a blank canvas.

Still stuck?

If you went through those five steps and realized you don’t actually know your colors, your fonts, your visual character… you don’t have a brand yet. And that’s not a Claude Design problem to solve. That’s a brand strategy problem.

Claude Design is a production tool. It builds from what you know. If you don’t know your brand, it’ll build something, just not something that’s actually yours.

Now You’re Ready to Build with Claude Design.

Once you upload your assets, Claude gets to work. This takes around 30 minutes.

When it’s done, it presents a first version of your brand system and checks in: does this look right, or does it need refining?

Hit “Need work” to make changes. Hit “Looks good” to approve.

Look at it critically. Check the colors, typography, overall feel. If something’s off, be specific: “The heading font feels too heavy — try something lighter.” “The accent color reads as orange, I need it more coral.”

Claude refines, then presents a second version.

Once you approve it, the system is locked in. Everything you build from this point uses your brand automatically.

What You Can Make With It.

From inside the design workspace you can create slide decks, landing pages, one-pagers, interactive prototypes, and social graphics. You work through conversation — describe what you want, Claude builds it on the canvas, you give feedback in chat or by clicking directly on elements. It refines. You iterate.

When you’re done, export as PDF, PPTX, standalone HTML, or send directly to Canva.

And if you want to go further — hand the design off to Claude Code, and it builds you an actual live website from it. No developer. No brief lost in translation. The design becomes the spec, and the spec becomes the site.

The Exact Prompt I Used to set up my AI Meets Girlboss brand system

Copy this, fill in the brackets, paste it into the company name and blurb field:

[Your publication name]: [One-sentence description of what you make and who for].

Visual identity: [Primary color or hex], [Secondary/neutral color], [Accent color].
Fonts: [Heading font] for headings, [Body font] for body text.
Style: [Adjective] and [adjective] — always [feel you want], never [feel you don't want].
Audience: [Who they are in one clause].

Mine looked like this:

AI Meets Girlboss: a Substack for ambitious women building 
personal brands with AI — visual, witty, and stylish.

Visual identity: #E32D91 (Girlboss Pink), #FDF2F8 (Flamingo Tint), 
#9C1F68 (Dark Hot Pink).
Fonts: Urbanist Bold for headings, Urbanist Regular for body.
Style: Mixed-media fashion illustration — always warm and editorial, 
never generic or corporate.
Audience: Solopreneurs and creators who want visual brand systems 
without the design background.

That’s it. 30 minutes later, I had a working brand system.

Brand Board Generator Prompt

Once your Claude Design system is set up, paste this prompt directly into the same Claude Design conversation. No manual input needed, because Claude already knows your brand.

You have just built my brand design system. 
Now use everything you know about my brand to produce a 
complete, production-ready HTML brand board.

Do not ask me for colors, fonts, or brand details — 
pull them directly from the design system you just created.

Build a single, self-contained HTML brand board page 
with these sections:

1. LOGO — 3 sizes on light and dark backgrounds
2. COLOR PALETTE — swatch cards with hex values and usage rules
3. TYPOGRAPHY — H1/H2/H3/body/accent examples written in my brand voice
4. CTA BUTTONS — primary, outline, and dark variants with hover states
5. EMOJIS & BRAND SIGNATURES — core emojis labeled, sign-off styled
6. COMPONENT LIBRARY — pill tags, callout boxes, numbered cards

LAYOUT RULES:
- Single scrollable HTML page, editorial and airy
- CSS Grid layout
- Sections alternate white and off-white backgrounds
- Generous whitespace throughout
- All sample copy must reflect my brand voice — no Lorem ipsum
- No pure black, no colors outside my defined palette
- No frameworks, no CDN scripts

OUTPUT: A complete self-contained HTML file.
All CSS inside a style block. 
Only Google Fonts allowed via @import.
Three Things Worth Uploading When You Think You Have Nothing.

Even if you’ve never made a brand guide, you probably have these:

A screenshot of a design you love. From any brand, any post, any app. “Make it feel like this” is a legitimate brief.

Your Substack or website URL. Claude reads it and extracts what’s already there — even if you hate how it currently looks.

Three brands you admire and three you’d never want to be compared to. Paste them into a text file. It’s faster to define yourself by contrast than from scratch.

The Honest Verdict on Claude Design.

The output quality when you give it a strong brief is genuinely impressive. For anyone with a brand system ready, it removes weeks of back-and-forth and makes you dangerous on your own.

The bigger picture is hard to ignore. Claude Design looks like a precursor to native image generation inside Claude. A tool that already builds brand systems and prototypes, gaining the ability to generate images natively — that’s not a design assistant anymore. That’s a full creative studio in one conversation.

Two things to know before you dive in.

It uses a lot of tokens. More than you’d expect. Even a clean session with a brand guide and a URL will make a dent in your monthly limit. Plan your sessions, don’t experiment freely and expect tokens left for everything else.

And if you don’t have a brand yet, Claude Design won’t create one for you. Strategy first, always.
